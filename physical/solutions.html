2. Concentration Terms

Important Terms
  • Molarity (M): Moles of solute per litre of solution. Temperature-dependent.
  • Molality (m): Moles of solute per kg of solvent. Temperature-independent.
  • Mole Fraction (X): Moles of component / Total moles
  • Mass % : (Mass of solute / Mass of solution) × 100
  • ppm: Parts per million
M = (w × 1000) / (M_w × V)
m = (w × 1000) / (M_w × W_solvent)
Mains Trap

Molarity temperature-dependent hai (volume change hota hai)

Molality temperature-independent hai (mass change nahi hota)

3. Raoult's Law

For Volatile Solute-Solvent (Binary Solution)

P_total = P_A + P_B

P_A = P_A° × X_A (Partial vapour pressure of A)

P_B = P_B° × X_B (Partial vapour pressure of B)

P_total = P_A° X_A + P_B° X_B

3.1 Vapour Phase Composition

Y_A = P_A / P_total
Y_B = P_B / P_total
For Non-Volatile Solute

P_total = P_A° × X_A (solute ka vapour pressure zero)

Relative lowering of vapour pressure = (P_A° − P_A) / P_A° = X_B

3.2 Raoult's Law for Non-Ideal Solutions

TypeDeviationΔH_mixExample
PositiveP > P_Raoult+veEthanol + Acetone
NegativeP < P_Raoult−veAcetone + Chloroform
Mains Trap
  • Positive deviation: Weaker unlike forces (A-B)
  • Negative deviation: Stronger unlike forces (A-B)
  • Minimum boiling azeotrope: Positive deviation
  • Maximum boiling azeotrope: Negative deviation

4. Colligative Properties

Definition

Aise properties jo solute particles ki number par depend karti hain, nature par nahi.

4.1 Relative Lowering of Vapour Pressure

(P° − P) / P° = X_solute = n / (n + N)

For dilute solution: (P° − P) / P° = n / N = w × M / (m × W)

4.2 Elevation of Boiling Point

ΔT_b = i × K_b × m

Jahan K_b = molal elevation constant, m = molality, i = Van't Hoff factor

4.3 Depression of Freezing Point

ΔT_f = i × K_f × m

Jahan K_f = molal depression constant

4.4 Osmotic Pressure

π = i × C × R × T
π = i × (n/V) × R × T

Jahan C = molarity, R = 0.0821 L·atm/K·mol, T = temperature (K)

5. Van't Hoff Factor (i)

Definition

i = (Observed colligative property) / (Calculated colligative property)

i = (Normal molar mass) / (Abnormal molar mass)

TypeiExample
No change1Glucose, Urea
Dissociation> 1NaCl (i = 2), CaCl₂ (i = 3)
Association< 1Benzoic acid in benzene (i = 0.5)
Mains Trap
  • Dissociation: i = 1 + (n−1)α
  • Association: i = 1 − (1 − 1/n)α
  • Dimerization (n = 2): i = 1 − α/2

6. Henry's Law

Statement

Gas ki solubility liquid mein uske partial pressure ke directly proportional hoti hai.

P = K_H × X

P = K_H × X_gas

Jahan K_H = Henry's law constant, X_gas = mole fraction of gas in solution

Mains Trap
  • K_H temperature ke saath badhta hai (solubility decrease)
  • Higher K_H → Lower solubility
  • Application: Scuba diving, Soft drinks, Oxygen tanks

7. Osmotic Pressure & Osmosis

Osmosis

Solvent molecules ka movement semi-permeable membrane ke through, higher concentration se lower concentration ki taraf.

Osmotic Pressure

Minimum pressure jo osmosis ko rokne ke liye apply karni padti hai.

π = iCRT

8. Abnormal Molar Mass

Cause
  • Dissociation: i > 1, observed molar mass < normal
  • Association: i < 1, observed molar mass > normal
i = Normal molar mass / Observed molar mass
Mains Trap
  • NaCl → i = 2 (Na⁺ + Cl⁻)
  • CaCl₂ → i = 3 (Ca²⁺ + 2Cl⁻)
  • K₄[Fe(CN)₆] → i = 5

Solved Numericals — Solutions

🧮 Type 1: Molarity Calculation

Q: Find molarity of solution containing 5.85 g NaCl in 500 mL solution. (M(NaCl) = 58.5)

  1. Moles of NaCl = 5.85 / 58.5 = 0.1 mol
  2. Volume = 500 mL = 0.5 L
  3. M = moles / volume = 0.1 / 0.5
  4. M = 0.2 M
🧮 Type 2: Molality Calculation

Q: Find molality of solution containing 4 g NaOH in 500 g water. (M(NaOH) = 40)

  1. Moles of NaOH = 4 / 40 = 0.1 mol
  2. Mass of solvent = 500 g = 0.5 kg
  3. m = moles / mass of solvent (kg) = 0.1 / 0.5
  4. m = 0.2 mol/kg
🧮 Type 3: Raoult's Law — Vapour Pressure

Q: P°_A = 100 mm Hg, P°_B = 200 mm Hg, X_A = 0.4. Find P_total.

  1. X_B = 1 − 0.4 = 0.6
  2. P_A = P°_A × X_A = 100 × 0.4 = 40 mm
  3. P_B = P°_B × X_B = 200 × 0.6 = 120 mm
  4. P_total = 40 + 120 = 160 mm Hg
🧮 Type 4: Relative Lowering of Vapour Pressure

Q: P° = 100 mm, P = 90 mm. Find mole fraction of solute.

  1. (P° − P) / P° = (100 − 90) / 100 = 0.1
  2. X_solute = 0.1
  3. X_solute = 0.1
🧮 Type 5: Elevation of Boiling Point

Q: Find ΔTb for 0.1 m glucose solution. (Kb = 0.52 K·kg/mol)

  1. Glucose → No dissociation → i = 1
  2. ΔTb = i × Kb × m
  3. ΔTb = 1 × 0.52 × 0.1
  4. ΔTb = 0.052 K
🧮 Type 6: Depression of Freezing Point

Q: Find ΔTf for 0.1 m NaCl solution. (Kf = 1.86 K·kg/mol)

  1. NaCl → i = 2
  2. ΔTf = i × Kf × m
  3. ΔTf = 2 × 1.86 × 0.1
  4. ΔTf = 0.372 K
🧮 Type 7: Osmotic Pressure

Q: Find osmotic pressure of 0.1 M glucose solution at 300 K. (R = 0.0821 L·atm/K·mol)

  1. π = iCRT
  2. π = 1 × 0.1 × 0.0821 × 300
  3. π = 2.463 atm
🧮 Type 8: Van't Hoff Factor (Dissociation)

Q: Find i for 0.1 M CaCl₂ if α = 0.8.

  1. CaCl₂ → Ca²⁺ + 2Cl⁻ → n = 3
  2. i = 1 + (n−1)α = 1 + (3−1) × 0.8
  3. i = 1 + 1.6
  4. i = 2.6
🧮 Type 9: Van't Hoff Factor (Association)

Q: Benzoic acid dimerizes in benzene with α = 0.6. Find i.

  1. Dimerization → n = 2
  2. i = 1 − (1 − 1/n)α = 1 − (1 − 1/2) × 0.6
  3. i = 1 − 0.5 × 0.6 = 1 − 0.3
  4. i = 0.7
🧮 Type 10: Henry's Law

Q: KH = 100 bar, partial pressure of gas = 10 bar. Find mole fraction of gas in solution.

  1. P = KH × X
  2. 10 = 100 × X
  3. X = 10 / 100
  4. X = 0.1
