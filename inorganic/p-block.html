p-Block Elements

Group 13-18, Hydrides, Oxides, Interhalogen, Noble Gases, PYQ Analysis

1. General Introduction

p-Block Kya Hai?
  • Group 13 to 18 ke elements
  • Inka last electron p-orbital mein jaata hai
  • General configuration: ns² np¹⁻⁶

1.1 Groups

GroupNameElements
13Boron FamilyB, Al, Ga, In, Tl
14Carbon FamilyC, Si, Ge, Sn, Pb
15Nitrogen FamilyN, P, As, Sb, Bi
16Oxygen FamilyO, S, Se, Te, Po
17HalogensF, Cl, Br, I, At
18Noble GasesHe, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn
Mains Trap
  • Group 15 → ns² np³ (half-filled, stable)
  • Group 18 → ns² np⁶ (full-filled, stable)

2. Group 13 – Boron Family

2.1 General Properties

  • B → Metalloid, baaki metals
  • Oxidation State: +3 (common), +1 (inert pair effect, heavier elements)
  • Inert Pair Effect: Tl⁺ > Tl³⁺ (stability)

2.2 Important Compounds

CompoundFormulaUse
BoraxNa₂B₄O₇·10H₂OGlass, soap
Boric AcidH₃BO₃Antiseptic
DiboraneB₂H₆Rocket fuel
AlumKAl(SO₄)₂·12H₂OWater purification
AlCl₃AlCl₃Friedel-Crafts catalyst

2.3 Diborane (B₂H₆) – Important

Key Points
  • Banana Bond (3-center-2-electron bond)
  • 2 Banana bonds, 4 terminal B–H bonds
  • B → sp³ hybridised

3. Group 14 – Carbon Family

3.1 General Properties

  • C → Non-metal, Si, Ge → Metalloids, Sn, Pb → Metals
  • Oxidation State: +4 (common), +2 (inert pair effect)
  • Catenation: C > Si > Ge > Sn > Pb

3.2 Allotropes of Carbon

TypeStructureProperty
Diamondsp³, 3D networkHardest, insulator
Graphitesp², layeredSoft, conductor
FullereneC₆₀, footballSemiconductor
Mains Trap
  • CO → Neutral oxide, toxic
  • SiO₂ → Acidic oxide
  • Pb⁴⁺ → Strong oxidising agent

4. Group 15 – Nitrogen Family

4.1 General Properties

  • N, P → Non-metals, As, Sb → Metalloids, Bi → Metal
  • Oxidation State: −3, +3, +5
  • N₂ → Inert (triple bond)

4.2 Hydride Stability

NH₃ > PH₃ > AsH₃ > SbH₃ > BiH₃ (Stability decreases)

4.3 Allotropes of Phosphorus

TypeStructureReactivity
White PP₄ (tetrahedral)Most reactive, poisonous
Red PPolymericLess reactive
Black PLayeredLeast reactive
Mains Trap
  • N₂ → Triple bond (N≡N), very stable
  • White P → Poisonous, glows in dark

5. Group 16 – Oxygen Family

5.1 General Properties

  • O, S, Se → Non-metals, Te → Metalloid, Po → Radioactive
  • Oxidation State: −2, +2, +4, +6
  • O → Most abundant element in earth's crust

5.2 Important Compounds

CompoundFormulaUse
H₂O₂H₂O₂Bleaching agent
H₂SO₄H₂SO₄King of chemicals
SO₂SO₂Bleaching, preservative
O₃O₃Ozone layer
Mains Trap
  • H₂O₂ → Both oxidising and reducing agent
  • O₃ → Bent shape, paramagnetic
  • H₂O₂ mein O ka oxidation state = −1

6. Group 17 – Halogens

6.1 General Properties

  • F, Cl, Br, I → Non-metals, At → Radioactive
  • Oxidation State: −1, +1, +3, +5, +7
  • F → Most electronegative, no positive oxidation state

6.2 Hydride Properties

PropertyOrder
Acidic StrengthHI > HBr > HCl > HF
Boiling PointHF > HI > HBr > HCl
StabilityHF > HCl > HBr > HI

6.3 Interhalogen Compounds

  • Types: AX, AX₃, AX₅, AX₇
  • Examples: ClF, BrF₃, IF₅, IF₇
  • Most stable: ClF
  • F hamesha terminal position pe hota hai
Mains Trap
  • F₂ → Most reactive, strongest oxidising agent
  • HF → Weak acid (H-bonding), attacks glass
  • HI → Strongest acid among halides

7. Group 18 – Noble Gases

7.1 General Properties

  • He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn
  • Inert (full-filled orbitals)
  • Oxidation State: 0 (mostly), Xe → +2, +4, +6, +8

7.2 Xenon Compounds

CompoundXe Oxidation StateHybridisationShape

Mains Trap
  • He → No compound (inert)
  • Xe → Maximum compounds
  • XeF₄ → Square planar

Mains Traps — p-Block Elements

1. Inert Pair Effect

Tl⁺ > Tl³⁺ (Group 13)

Pb²⁺ > Pb⁴⁺ (Group 14)

Sn⁴⁺ > Sn²⁺

2. Hydride Stability

NH₃ > PH₃ > AsH₃ > SbH₃ > BiH₃

HF > HCl > HBr > HI

H₂O > H₂S > H₂Se > H₂Te

3. Boiling Point (Hydrides)

HF > HI > HBr > HCl

H-bonding ke karan HF ka boiling point sabse zyada

4. Acidic Strength (Halogen Hydrides)

HI > HBr > HCl > HF

5. Oxoacid Strength

Same oxidation state: HClO > HBrO > HIO

Different ox. state: HClO₄ > HClO₃ > HClO₂ > HClO

6. Catenation Order

C > Si > Ge > Sn > Pb

7. Interhalogen Compounds

ClF → Most stable interhalogen

F → Always terminal position

8. Diborane (B₂H₆)

2 Banana bonds, 4 terminal B–H bonds

B → sp³ hybridised

9. Noble Gases

XeF₄ → Square Planar

XeF₂ → Linear

XeF₆ → Distorted Octahedral

10. Oxidising Power

F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂

Quick Revision — p-Block Elements (Last 10 Minutes)

📐 Key Points
  • Group 13: B, Al, Ga, In, Tl. B₂H₆ (Banana bonds)
  • Group 14: C, Si, Ge, Sn, Pb. Catenation C > Si
  • Group 15: N, P, As, Sb, Bi. White P most reactive
  • Group 16: O, S, Se, Te, Po. H₂O₂ (O = −1)
  • Group 17: F, Cl, Br, I, At. F₂ strongest oxidiser
  • Group 18: He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn. XeF₄ square planar
📊 Key Orders
  • Acidic Strength: HI > HBr > HCl > HF
  • Boiling Point: HF > HI > HBr > HCl
  • Oxidising Power: F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂
  • Stability: HF > HCl > HBr > HI
  • Catenation: C > Si > Ge > Sn > Pb
⚠️ Key Traps
  • Inert Pair Effect: Tl⁺ > Tl³⁺, Pb²⁺ > Pb⁴⁺
  • B₂H₆ → 2 Banana bonds
  • H₂O₂ → O ka oxidation state = −1
  • O₃ → Bent, paramagnetic
  • ClF → Most stable interhalogen
  • XeF₄ → Square planar
🧬 Hybridisation & Shapes
  • XeF₂ → sp³d → Linear
  • XeF₄ → sp³d² → Square Planar
  • XeF₆ → sp³d³ → Distorted Octahedral
  • ClF₃ → sp³d → T-Shape
  • BrF₅ → sp³d² → Square Pyramidal
🔑 Oxoacids
  • HClO > HBrO > HIO (same ox. state)
  • HClO₄ > HClO₃ > HClO₂ > HClO
  • H₂SO₄ → King of chemicals
💡 Noble Gases
  • He → No compound
  • Xe → Maximum compounds
  • XeF₄ → Square planar
